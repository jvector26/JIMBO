Points added = how many points he has added to his team's scoring margin so far, compared with an average player in the same minutes (season rating × possessions ÷ 100). So it rewards playing well and playing a lot: a big rating in a few minutes won't climb this list. Season rating = points per 100 possessions vs an average player, from this season's box score only: nothing from past seasons or the projection, and nothing pulled toward average, so small samples swing a lot. Sorting by season rating lists players averaging 15+ minutes who have played at least half their team's games. Lineup results aren't in it. Updated nightly.

    How it works. Each player's rating is points per 100 possessions over an average player: box-score evidence for twelve skills plus hustle stats (loose balls, 3-point contests), weighted by how much each has historically moved scoring margin, plus what lineup results show beyond the box score (raw lineup results count 60% on offense and 80% on defense, where past seasons predicted best; the lineup sliders scale from there), then adjusted for age (the age curve has its own slider). Minutes come from each player's history and age; team minutes always total 82 games of 240. The forecast blends the model with the betting market and roster age, the mix that best predicted 330 past preseasons, with the model and market shares adding to 100% (average miss 8.2 wins, against 8.4 for the betting line alone and 8.7 for the model alone). Weight sliders change the model; at the default mix the forecast moves about 0.35 wins for every model win (the model-vs-Vegas slider changes that). Playoff games use playoff minutes (stars play more, the rotation shortens; half weight) and a bigger home-court edge, 4 points, as in past playoffs. In season, a team playing the second night of a back-to-back loses 2 points in that game's odds and the season simulation (2015-25 games: about 1.7 from fatigue, the rest from regulars sitting). The accuracy box replays 2014-15 through 2025-26 with each season's ratings predicting the next using who actually played. Rosters and lines as of .

    Role sets the minutes the model starts from (starter, rotation, limited, out of the rotation); the team's minutes are re-shared around it. A team always has five starters: move a starter down and the next man up (by the depth chart, then projected minutes) starts in his place; move someone up and the chart starter with the fewest projected minutes goes to the rotation. Games and minutes you type yourself take priority. During the season, minutes actually played gradually take over.
